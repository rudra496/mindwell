# Team SignTalk: Translating Sign Language to Speech with a Smart Glove — Our Journey from SUST to International Recognition

Communication is a fundamental human right, yet many deaf and speech-impaired people still face daily communication barriers in classrooms, hospitals, public offices, and workplaces. Team SignTalk started with one core question: **can we build low-cost assistive technology in Bangladesh that turns hand signs into understandable speech quickly and reliably?**

At Shahjalal University of Science and Technology (SUST), our interdisciplinary team explored that question through prototyping, user testing, and repeated iteration. The result was **SignTalk**, a smart glove that captures hand and finger motion and translates those gestures into text and audio output.

## Why SignTalk matters

Traditional communication gaps are not just inconvenient; they can become safety issues during emergencies and critical barriers for education and employment. SignTalk aims to reduce that gap by supporting faster communication between sign-language users and non-sign-language users.

Our goal was not to “replace” sign language or deaf culture. Instead, we wanted to provide an optional bridge tool in contexts where interpreters are not available.

## Technical approach

SignTalk combines hardware sensing, embedded processing, and software interpretation:

## Hardware layer

- Flex and motion sensing to capture finger bends and hand orientation
- Microcontroller-based processing for low-latency signal handling
- Wireless data transfer for output display and speech generation

## Signal and model layer

- Sensor calibration pipeline for gesture consistency
- Filtering and normalization to reduce noisy readings
- Rule-based and pattern-based mapping from gesture vectors to text labels

## Output layer

- Text output on connected device screen
- Speech synthesis for real-time audio communication

The technical stack evolved through each prototype cycle, but the principle stayed the same: **fast gesture recognition with practical usability in real environments**.

## Collaboration and team learning

SignTalk was never a solo build. It required close collaboration across embedded systems, interface design, testing, pitching, and documentation. Teamwork turned out to be one of the strongest advantages of the project.

At SUST, we learned to move from “cool idea” to “tested product narrative”: define user need, build prototype, collect feedback, improve, repeat.
